What breaks confidence

Top blockers, ranked by frequency

  1. All-Alabaster gallery / no on-skin swatches across tones. Near-universal, and the single biggest blocker. Medium, olive and deep prospects can’t picture the product on themselves at all.
  2. Shade pickable only from a jar image + one line of undertone text. Can’t judge cool/warm/neutral — acute for olive and deep.
  3. Only a tone-category filter, no true undertone quiz. The exact dimension that decides a match is missing.
  4. No customer review photos (UGC). Younger, online-native prospects expect Amazon / Rare-Beauty-style proof.
  5. “Light-medium, buildable” reads as low coverage. Coverage-seekers worry it’s just a tinted moisturizer.
  6. Fine print “some shifting may occur in transit” — spooks shade-anxious buyers about getting the wrong color.
  7. No skin-type guidance or mature-skin proof. Combination and 55+ prospects can’t tell if it suits them or settles into lines.

What builds confidence

Lean into these

  • Review volume (4.46★, 53k) — the top builder across the board; instantly de-risks a new brand. They want it filterable, with photos, from people their age/tone.
  • Free shade matching — the single biggest reassurance for medium/olive/deep prospects. It’s your best safety net, and today it’s buried.
  • Free returns / exchanges — universal risk remover that makes trying it feel safe.
  • Clinical / non-comedogenic / 24h-hydration badges + the Sodium Hyaluronate callout — make the “nourishing” claim feel real, especially for mature and dry skin.
  • FAQ self-selection (vs tinted moisturizer / vs traditional foundation) — genuinely helps BB-cream and coverage shoppers place the product.
The deliverable

Prioritized PDP-redesign fixes

Apply across all product pages. Ordered by leverage.

  1. On-skin swatches across the full tone range — non-negotiable. Show every shade on a real, matching complexion, not just in-jar. Highest-leverage fix; it’s what unlocks the medium and deep segments. Worth more than any copy change.
  2. Build a real undertone shade-finder (cool/warm/neutral → a specific shade), and surface “free shade matching” prominently next to the selector — it’s already your best safety net, just buried.
  3. Add filterable reviews with photos, filterable by skin tone / age / undertone. You have the volume (53k) — make it shoppable and representative.
  4. Lead deep / rich skin in imagery and reviews, not just the fair end. The all-Alabaster gallery silently tells deeper-tone shoppers they’re an afterthought — and they leave for Fenty.
  5. Show the finish, don’t just claim it — a short on-skin application clip + a built-up before/after. Resolves the “is light-medium enough coverage?” doubt and justifies $46.
  6. Add skin-type and concern cues (oily/combo/dry; fine lines; ashiness/oxidation for rich tones) — small copy adds that convert the “will this work for ME” hesitators.
  7. Reframe the transit fine print so it doesn’t read as “the shade might arrive wrong.”
Biggest single thing missing

Their words, ranked

  • On-skin swatches of my shade on skin like mine — especially medium / olive / deep (~15 of 20).
  • A true undertone quiz (cool/warm/neutral → a specific shade), not just tone buckets.
  • Customer review photos across tones.
  • Coverage proof — a before/after built up to medium; does it sit in lines.
  • Skin-type guidance and mature-skin wear.
