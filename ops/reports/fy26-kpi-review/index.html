Format Guidance — Hierarchy & Terminology
Two changes to the framework before we re-circulate this: 1) Terminology — "Output KPI" → "Outcome KPI". There's a lot of confusion across the deck: people are submitting activities and labeling them "Output KPIs." The word "outcome" is sharper. It forces the question "what is the result?" rather than "what did I produce?". Same concept, clearer word. 2) Structure — Objectives, not Big Rocks, anchor a person's KPIs. The hierarchy is: Big Rock (company-level priority — there are 6) ↓ Objective — the person's own vision / mission area. Each person should have a small number (typically 2–3), not one per KPI. ↓ Input KPIs — activities that ladder up to the objective. There can be many under a single objective. Outcome KPIs — how we measure whether the objective was actually hit. The Input vs. Outcome test: "If you couldn't look at outcomes at all for the entire year, which activities would you do that would make you 100% confident the objective gets hit?" — those are your Input KPIs. Outcome KPIs are how we measure success at EOY. Examples of the confusion in current submissions: • "Conduct weekly store-level sales review" — this is an Input (activity). • "Retail division hits $30.3M in 2026" — this is an Outcome (result). • "Photoshoots and events are executed smoothly" — this isn't either. It's an aspiration. Outcomes need a number or a defined gate. Why this matters: bonuses are paid against these. Outcome KPIs are the unambiguous definition of done; Input KPIs are the work plan that earns the outcome.
