Wide Awake Brightening Concealer / Neutralizer

The only creamy-pot concealer that actively brightens — not just covers.
Date 2026-05-02 Launch type new-product-existing-category Tier 1 Launch September 2026 Author /strategic-brief skill draft

Consumer Insights

Sample profile: Typeform skews 91% age 46+; 64% named Jones Road or Bobbi Brown as their current concealer/neutralizer brand — this is largely a customer read, not net-new audience. ListenLabs runs broader (25–44 + 45–64 mix).

What She Wants

  • Top benefit appeal (Typeform T2B 4–5): No creasing / won't settle into fine lines (100%) · Easy to apply (96%) · Coverage of imperfections (93%) · Coverage of dark circles (92%) · Lightening under eyes (90%)
  • Top features for current users: non-dry/non-cakey (57%), natural finish (55%), lightweight feel (46%) — comfort beats coverage as the deciding feature
  • Creamy texture indexes +10pts higher among current concealer users (32%) vs non-users (10%) — confirms the creamy-pot bet
  • Dark circles dominate — 80% of LL users cite as primary goal; 93% of LL users apply concealer under their eyes

What She's Worried About

  • #1 non-user barrier: "Worried it will look cakey" (55%) + "I didn't fully understand the product" (30%) — the path in for non-users is reassurance, not coverage
  • Combo confusion: 25% of LL respondents don't know what a neutralizer is; 66% feel "lost" combining concealer + neutralizer

Sub-Tension (from customer-intelligence)

  • JRB Neutralizer Pencil customers love the result ("Finally found something that corrects my dark circles! This stuff is magic" — 5★ Junip) but complain about the format ("drags on my eyelids," "pulls on skin" — cross-product pencil dryness flagged). Wide Awake's pot + creamy formula directly resolves a known JRB customer pain.

Potential Barriers

  • Cakey reputation owns the category. Tarte (8 mentions, incl. Shape Tape — the heaviest tier), NARS, and Benefit all market "full coverage" but are the brands consumers fear when they say "cakey." JRB will be lumped in unless Wide Awake's lighter-feel claim leads — comfort first, coverage second.
  • "Color correcting" doesn't translate. Lowest T2B benefit at 59% (LL). Even category users don't fully understand what a neutralizer does — the concept is abstract, the brand language fails. Re-frame as outcome ("brightens what makeup can't fix") not mechanism.
  • Cannibalization risk. JRB customers already use The Face Pencil + The Neutralizer Pencil. Wide Awake must clearly play a distinct role (the creamy pot for women who don't like pencil drag) or it splits the category instead of growing it.
  • Active-brightening claim must be defensible. R.E.M, Tarte, and others bury "brightening" as soft sub-benefits. JRB's Plant Complex is the real point of difference, but "actively brightens" needs TEWL/Consumer Perception data front and center to land.

Primary + Secondary Target

  • Primary: JRB-aware women 45–65 who already buy The Face Pencil but find the Neutralizer Pencil drags on her eyelids. Wants a creamier, more comfortable under-eye solution that does more than cover (it brightens too). 91% of TF respondents fit this demo; 64% already in the JRB/Bobbi Brown universe.
  • Secondary: "Cakey-fearful" non-users (55% non-user barrier) currently using drugstore concealer (Maybelline 5, e.l.f. 5, LA Colors 3) or nothing. Convertible through experience claims (weightless, no-crease, easy) — coverage claims come second for this group.

Key Product Differentiator

  • The only creamy-pot concealer that actively brightens — not just covers. The Plant Complex (marshmallow root + rice bran + licorice root) visibly fades darkness so coverage gets to do less work each wear. Competitors split the category between heavy-matte coverage (NARS, Benefit, Tarte Shape Tape) and skincare-dewy with no coverage (Glossier, Tarte Maracuja, R.E.M.). Nobody combines real medium-to-full coverage + active brightening complex + creamy comfort in a pot.

Product Positioning Statement

Only Wide Awake delivers real coverage that actively brightens — a creamy pot formula with a Plant Complex (marshmallow root + rice bran + licorice root) that wakes up tired eyes without ever feeling heavy or cakey.

Core Message / Campaign Idea

  • The first concealer you don't have to choose with. Coverage and brightening, real and weightless, full and creamy — Wide Awake collapses every trade-off concealer makes you make.
  • Stop covering. Start brightening. Position "actively brightens" as a category callout — the brands that say "color correcting" are talking technical. Wide Awake talks about what tired eyes actually need.
  • Tension to exploit: the customer who's tried every concealer and still hasn't found one she trusts under her eyes. She blames her eyes — it's the formula. Wide Awake names that.
  • Verbatim flip (TF non-user barrier): "Worried it'll look cakey" — turn the #1 objection into the campaign promise.

Other Product Differentiators

For use in additional assets (ads, PDP, email, PR)

  • Pot format vs. JRB pencil drag — directly addresses cross-product complaints about The Neutralizer Pencil ("drags on my eyelids," "pulls on skin" — Junip 1–3★ flagged in customer-intelligence). Pot + creamy = no drag, no pulling.
  • Buildable medium-to-full coverage that works with any application method. TF: 66% blend with fingers, 31% brush, 1.6% sponge. LL: 41% sponge, 39% fingers, 20% brush. Pot format earns its keep across the spectrum.
  • System play. Junip: "This neutralizer pencil is the magical cosmetic item I never knew I needed… combined with the face pencils." Wide Awake completes the under-eye system in pot format. Cross-sell with The Face Pencil + The Neutralizer Pencil.
  • 39 SKUs (30 concealer + 9 neutralizer) — outpaces Benefit (6), Bobbi Brown (12), Tarte Maracuja (21). Tied with NARS (30). Shade-range inclusivity as prestige table-stakes.
  • Visual distinction at point-of-use. Bone cap = concealer, pink cap = neutralizer. Solves the combo confusion (66% of LL users feel "lost") without requiring an education campaign.
  • 5g shallow ALU jar — premium feel, retail-friendly, hazmat-free.
  • Testing plan supports the brightening claim. TEWL / Corneometry · Consumer Perception · Comedogenicity. Use Consumer Perception data inline on PDP and in proof-of-claim ads.

Key Ingredients

  • Plant Complex (marshmallow root + rice bran + licorice root) — Botanical actives that visibly brighten darkness/discoloration, soothe redness, and even tone. The mechanism behind "brightens, not just covers."
  • Shea Butter — Skin-softening emollient. Keeps formula creamy, comfortable, never heavy.
  • Squalane — Plant-derived humectant. Locks in moisture without grease.
  • Vitamin E (Tocopherol) — Antioxidant. Protects against visible signs of aging.
