Verdict at a glance

  • Audience saturation (primary cause): Meta and Google prospecting (YouTube + Demand Gen) both saw CPA rise ~2.4× since Jan 2024 — in lockstep. Prospecting is saturating symmetrically across platforms.
  • Price elasticity at the $40-50 impulse threshold (secondary, partial): Sale periods unlock big lifts. But JRB pricing is mid-pack vs Merit / Saie / Westman — not "too expensive" by category standards.
  • Macro / category headwinds (background): Decline started pre-Feb 2025. The price hike amplified — it didn't initiate.

Section 2.8 · Follow-up

Net-new reach isn't the bottleneck — converting it is

Pulled straight from the Meta Ads API: reach, frequency, and the new-vs-existing (prospecting) split. This tests Mandar's "net new reach is dropping" claim directly — and points the diagnosis at conversion, not reach.

Meta reach — net-new vs existing, with new-customer AOV
Black = net-new (prospecting). Gray = existing (retargeting). Pink line = new-customer AOV. Dashed marker = Feb 10, 2025 price increase.
Net-new (prospecting) reach vs net-new → first-purchase conversion
Bars = net-new accounts reached per month (left). Line = share of those reached who became first-time buyers (right).

What the reach data says

  • Net-new reach is 89-99% of all Meta reach every month — JRB's Meta account is overwhelmingly a prospecting machine, not a retargeting one.
  • It's growing, not shrinking: ~16-18M net-new accounts/month in early 2025 rose to 23-34M/month in Mar-May 2026.
  • Cost per 1,000 reached is flat-to-down: total CPMr went $113 (Jan 2024) → $79-103 (2026); net-new CPMr $98 (Feb 2025) → $69-91 (2026). Reaching new people got cheaper, not more expensive.
  • Frequency fell from ~6.0 to ~4.5 — Meta is spreading across more unique accounts, fewer times each. Not the signature of reach exhaustion.

The smoking gun

Net-new-reach → first-purchase conversion fell 2-4× — from ~0.11-0.16% in 2025 to ~0.03-0.06% in 2026. We're putting JRB in front of more new people and converting a far smaller fraction of them. That is exactly what a price/offer barrier looks like — which strengthens Mandar's underlying thesis even though the "reach is dropping" framing doesn't hold in the raw data.

Reconciliation needed: Mandar's declining "net new reach" red line likely measures net-new activations within bid caps (a cost-constrained metric), not raw reach. His screenshot + Malik's 90-day pull will confirm. Either way, the actionable conclusion is the same: the lever is conversion of the new audience (offer, price, landing page, creative), not finding more of them.

Section 2.4

Sale-period lift IS evidence of price elasticity

Mandar's strongest argument: when JRB discounts, NCs spike. The pattern is real and consistent — but it's also been there for two years. What's new is the smaller absolute audience the lifts pull from.

May 2026 Memorial Day lift was the most modest of the series. Two possible reads:

  • Smaller responsive audience: The base we lift from has shrunk; same elasticity × smaller pool = smaller absolute lift.
  • Less compelling discount: If May 2026 discount depth was lighter than May 2025, the lift would be expected to be smaller. Worth checking against the actual offer ladder.

Either read supports the case for testing more aggressive welcome offers — but neither validates a permanent list-price rollback.

The JRB Mini line ($19-24) is correctly priced for impulse but appears under-leveraged for paid acquisition.
