Verdict at a glance

  • Audience saturation (primary cause): Both Meta and Google show 2-5× CPA increases and 60-87% conversion volume collapses since Jan 2024.
  • Price elasticity at the $40-50 impulse threshold (secondary, partial): Sale periods unlock big lifts. But JRB pricing is mid-pack vs Merit / Saie / Westman — not "too expensive" by category standards.
  • Macro / category headwinds (background): Decline started pre-Feb 2025. The price hike amplified — it didn't initiate.

What to do

Launch the queued Intelligems "Offer Test · Apr 25" this week (zero setup cost) and build a Discovery Set funnel test in parallel. Pull Meta biddable-audience numbers from Malik to disambiguate audience-pool-size vs conversion-rate. Do not pursue a permanent price rollback — JRB is competitively priced and the lever to pull first is welcome-offer A/B, not list-price.

The hypothesis decomposed:

  1. Meta's biddable net new audience is shrinking.
  2. JRB's price point is the constraint — Meta can't find enough customers willing to convert at full price.
  3. The Memorial Day spike is pent-up demand from price-sensitive customers.
  4. Google does not show this pattern.
  5. The lever to test: bigger / better-targeted discount offers, not generic site-wide sales.

And Ben's framing

"Yea would be hard to pinpoint as the exact factor since there are so many other things that could have impacted it — but would be interesting to see the trend line." Ben Rosenwald · Slack DM · May 28, 2026

That's exactly what this analysis does — pulls the trend line, separates the price-hike signal from the surrounding noise, and assigns weight to each force.

Section 2.4

Sale-period lift IS evidence of price elasticity

Mandar's strongest argument: when JRB discounts, NCs spike. The pattern is real and consistent — but it's also been there for two years. What's new is the smaller absolute audience the lifts pull from.

May 2026 Memorial Day lift was the most modest of the series. Two possible reads:

  • Smaller responsive audience: The base we lift from has shrunk; same elasticity × smaller pool = smaller absolute lift.
  • Less compelling discount: If May 2026 discount depth was lighter than May 2025, the lift would be expected to be smaller. Worth checking against the actual offer ladder.

Either read supports the case for testing more aggressive welcome offers — but neither validates a permanent list-price rollback.

Section 2.6

JRB sits mid-pack on price

JRB is NOT priced out of line vs clean-beauty premium peers. Westman is higher; Merit and Saie are at parity; Tower 28 and Rare Beauty have built large audiences by pricing below $25.

The strategic read: JRB's pricing is consistent with the premium-clean-beauty tier. But the $38-46 unit prices sit above the $20-30 impulse-purchase threshold that drives Meta-paid first-time conversion at scale. Tower 28 and Rare Beauty have built large net-new audiences specifically by pricing below $25 — this is the impulse zone. The JRB Mini line ($19-24) is correctly priced for impulse but appears under-leveraged for paid acquisition.

Section 6

Data caveats

Meta Reach Live backfill. Polar's Meta Reach Live connector only has data for May 2026 (483M). Earlier months show 0 — used FB impressions as proxy for historical reach.
No Junip review text mining. Polar exposes Junip aggregates only (count, avg rating, recommendation %). To mine "expensive / price / worth it" mentions we need direct Junip MCP access.
No new-buyer demographic shift. Frequency Segmentation dimension doesn't slice new customers usefully (all NCs are "Newly Acquired" by definition). Would require a dedicated GA4 dive or cohort study.
Attribution noise. Northbeam first-time % stayed 70-87% throughout the period — useful for confirming Meta is still a NC channel but doesn't capture cross-channel cross-shopping.
